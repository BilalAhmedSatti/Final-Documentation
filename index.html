What is this documentation about?

This site explains how a person in Pakistan opens and uses an EMI e-money wallet (digital money app) — legally and safely. It is written so a new team member can understand the path before learning the jargon.

The one-sentence idea

Before someone can send or spend digital money, the company must prove who they are, check they are not banned, and only then give them a wallet number — after a short security wait.

The happy path above is called J1 (clean biometric). If fingerprint is impossible, or the customer is high risk, or something fails, other journeys (J2–J19) describe what happens instead.

Three things people mix up
  • Tracking ID = “your application is in progress” (like a ticket number).
  • Debit-blocked wallet (J3) = file opened, but the customer still cannot spend.
  • Wallet number = real account they can use — only after checks + 2-hour wait.
Wallet vs bank account

This product is an EMI wallet (electronic money). Money is safeguarded at a trustee bank. It is not a normal bank deposit and not a Roshan Digital Account (RDA).

SBP audit readiness

This page is for people who are new to banking compliance. It explains — in everyday language — what rules we follow, what “sanctions” means, what we must keep for State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) reviewers, and how to keep every KYC journey reconstructible so an audit does not fall apart.

In plain English

Opening a wallet is not only “fill a form.” It is a controlled process that can be replayed later like a black-box recording of a flight. When SBP (or an approved systems auditor) asks “show me this customer,” we must show the full story: who did what, when, with which evidence, and why the wallet was allowed — or refused.

Simple analogy: Airport security keeps camera footage, boarding passes, and screening logs. If something goes wrong, investigators rebuild the passenger’s path. KYC works the same way.

Important

This documentation explains how we design the product. It is not legal advice. Official circulars on sbp.org.pk always prevail.

1. What is an SBP audit here?

SBP (and firms on SBP-approved audit panels) check whether our EMI wallet follows Pakistan’s rules for e-money, digital onboarding, AML (anti-money laundering), and customer protection. They do not only read policy PDFs — they pick real cases and ask:

  1. Can you find this application by Tracking ID?
  2. Was identity proven the right way (fingerprint / Verisys / video)?
  3. Was the person screened against banned lists before money could move?
  4. Was the wallet number given only after all gates + the 2-hour wait?
  5. If something failed (NADRA down, sanctions hit), did the system stop safely?
  6. Can you show evidence, timestamps, and who approved special cases?
Pass mindset

Every live wallet should have a complete time-ordered story. Every decline should have a clear reason and a kept Tracking ID. Mystery gaps (“we approved it somehow”) are what get audits into trouble.

3. Sanctions — explained simply

What is a sanctions check?

Before (and after) someone uses the wallet, we check whether their name / identity matches people or groups that Pakistan or the UN have designated as banned or restricted. In our docs this is often called pre-screening, TFS (targeted financial sanctions), or “lists.”

Which lists?

  • UNSC — United Nations Security Council designated lists
  • ATA 1997 — Pakistan’s Anti-Terrorism Act proscribed lists
  • Plus designed watchlists / PEP (politically exposed persons) checks as part of risk

What can happen?

ResultWhat we doJourney
ClearContinue onboarding / keep operatingSpine → J1 / J2 …
Possible hitHold. Investigate internally. Do not tell the customer “you are on a list.”Hold → then clear or J5
True matchNo wallet. Written decline. Internal TFS / possible STR. Customer gets a safe generic message.J5 then J8
List service downWait / retry. Never mark “clear” because the checker was offline.J11
No tipping-off

The customer must not be told “you matched a sanctions list” or “we filed a suspicious transaction report.” That warning could help criminals destroy evidence. Decline messages stay truthful but generic. Internal reason codes can be richer than the customer-facing text.

What auditors will ask about sanctions

  • Did screening run before payment services were allowed?
  • Did we store the list version and timestamp with the decision?
  • If lists were down, did we fail closed (J11) instead of silently approving?
  • After the wallet is live, does monitoring continue (J10)?

4. The non‑negotiable product rules

These are the rules that most often decide whether a sample case looks compliant.

  1. Tracking ID is not a wallet. The case file opens first. The spendable wallet number comes late — after verification, screening, CDD, and cooling-off.
  2. Fail closed. If NADRA, lists, or OTP systems are down, we wait or retry (J11). We never invent a silent “approved.”
  3. One active wallet per CNIC with this EMI (J12). Returning customers are told clearly to log in; strangers do not get answers that help them probe who is a customer.
  4. Strongest identity proof first. NADRA biometric (fingerprint / face BV) is primary. Lower options (Verisys, debit-block, video) only for listed genuine reasons — not because someone is in a hurry (ladder).
  5. A selfie is not biometric verification. Live photo fights impersonation; NADRA BV proves identity against the national registry.
  6. Limits follow how strongly we verified. Example bands: Verisys ~ PKR 50,000 / month; biometric ~ PKR 400,000 commercial (pilot often 200,000); enhanced up to ~1,000,000 only with extra controls (limits, J13).
  7. 2-hour cooling-off before the wallet can operate after activation — and again on new device, contact change, or limit upgrade (activation).
  8. High risk needs extra work. HIGH CRP / PEP → more documents and senior approval before activate (J6).
  9. Agents never open wallets. They may do cash-in/out under rules; cash-in needs BV (J19).
  10. Keep the evidence. Applications, screening decisions, video KYC, and key artefacts are retained for the AML / EMI retention period (commonly spoken of as about 10 years in these docs).

8. What usually fails an audit sample

  • Wallet number issued before screening, BV/Verisys, or cooling-off
  • “Approved” while NADRA or sanctions lists were down
  • Second active wallet for the same CNIC
  • Verisys-only customer given biometric-tier limits without NADRA BV
  • Treating a selfie / liveness score as if it were NADRA BV
  • Agent channel created a wallet
  • No list version / timestamp on a screening decision
  • Customer message that tips off sanctions or STR
  • HIGH risk activated without senior approval trail
  • Missing evidence: photos, consents, or vendor references gone
  • Staff can override to “APPROVED” without named maker-checker reason

Capture & gates

When does the wallet number appear?

In plain English

The wallet number is given late. First prove identity, clear safety checks, finish the form, get senior approval if high risk — then wait 2 hours. Only then can the customer spend.

Four green lights (all required)

  1. Identity proven — fingerprint OK, or full Verisys bundle OK.
  2. Not on banned list — sanctions check clear (if the check is down, wait — do not approve).
  3. Form complete — name, address, purpose, and required ID fields collected.
  4. High risk approved — if risk is HIGH, a senior person must approve first (J6).
The 2-hour wait (cooling-off)

After those four lights are green, the app still waits 2 hours before money can move. Same wait applies when adding a new phone (J9), changing mobile/email/password (J14), or raising limits (J13).

Remember

  • Application number ≠ wallet number.
  • Debit-blocked (J3) is not a spending wallet.
  • Never skip the 2-hour wait for convenience.
  • Never auto-approve if NADRA or lists timed out.

Capture & gates

How do we prove identity? (the ladder)

In plain English

Always try fingerprint / face with NADRA first (best proof, higher limits). Only if that is truly impossible for an allowed reason, move to weaker options. You cannot skip down the ladder just because it is easier.

The complete domain picture for our multi-tenant, multi-region Digital Banking & EMI platform — accounting, regulation, KYC, payments, fees, risk, cards, reporting, and FX — in one place for the whole team.

In plain English

An EMI wallet is digital money. Customers hold a balance; we hold matching reserves at a trustee bank. Every payment moves value with double-entry bookkeeping. Every market (Pakistan → UAE → Saudi) has its own central bank rules, ID system, and payment rails — but the same eleven capability pillars.

1. Executive summary & domain scope

This specification synthesizes core banking capabilities, regulatory frameworks, compliance rules, financial accounting mechanics, and operational workflows into a single authoritative reference for the multi-tenant, multi-region platform.

Launch sequence

Pakistan (SBP) → UAE (CBUAE) → Saudi Arabia (SAMA).

  • Multi-tenant — one platform serving multiple institutions / brands with isolation.
  • Multi-region — country packs for ID, rails, tax, residency, and limits.
  • EMI / digital banking — e-money and payment services under central-bank licences (not a generic fintech toy).

2. Pillar 1 — Double-entry ledger (TigerBeetle + PostgreSQL)

In plain English

Money is never “created from thin air” inside the ledger. Every move is a pair: something is debited and something is credited by the same amount. Formula: sum of debits = sum of credits.

Non‑negotiable invariants

  1. Integer minor units only — store paisa / fils / cents as bigint (e.g. @platform/money). Floating point (float/double) is forbidden.
  2. Account types
    • Asset (↑ debit, ↓ credit) — e.g. commercial bank escrow balances we hold.
    • Liability (↑ credit, ↓ debit) — customer wallets (our obligation to the customer).
    • Equity / Income / Expense — fees revenue and operating costs.
  3. Reversals over edits — ledgers are append-only. Mistakes get an opposite reversing post — never delete or overwrite history.
  4. TigerBeetle — authoritative posting engine for balances and 2-phase holds (debits = credits). PostgreSQL keeps append-only journal metadata (ADR-0004).
Fail closed for money math

If a transfer cannot balance, do not post a one-sided entry. Hold, reverse, or leave in-flight for reconciliation — never “fix” the balance.

4. Pillar 3 — Onboarding, eKYC & compliance screening

In plain English

Nobody gets a working wallet until identity and sanctions checks finish safely. If a check times out or fails, the case goes to HOLD / MANUAL_REVIEW — it is never auto-approved “optimistically.”

Fail-closed rule

Screening timeout or eKYC failure → MANUAL_REVIEW / HOLD. An account is never auto-cleared optimistically. Same spirit as journeys J5, J11, and the SBP audit readiness page.

5. Pillar 4 — Payment rails, clearing & settlement sagas

External money movement uses national instant rails through SPI adapters.

JurisdictionInstant railRTGS / clearingSPI adapter
PakistanRaast (SBP)PRISMPayoutRailPort (Raast)
UAEAani (CBUAE)UAEFTPayoutRailPort (Aani)
Saudi ArabiaSarie (SAMA)SARIE RTGSPayoutRailPort (Sarie)

Transfer saga lifecycle

  1. Authorize — Lock customer funds on TigerBeetle (pending hold).
  2. Execute — Submit payload to the national rail via SPI adapter.
  3. Settle / confirm
    • SUCCESS — Commit hold → Debit customer wallet, Credit outbound settlement account.
    • FAILURE — Void hold → funds return to customer.
    • TIMEOUT — Leave IN_FLIGHT; run automated reconciliation query.

6. Pillar 5 — Dynamic fee engine & tax withholding

Pricing (service 10) calculates revenue fees and government tax.

Fee structures

  • Fixed — flat amount (e.g. 15 PKR ATM fee).
  • Percentage — e.g. 0.5% international spend.
  • Slab — tiered ranges (0–25k free, above that fee applies).

Tax withholding (examples)

  • Pakistan (FBR) — WHT for non-filers + provincial sales tax (about 13–16% SST/PST).
  • Saudi Arabia (ZATCA) — 15% VAT on processing fees.
  • UAE (FTA) — 5% VAT on processing fees.
Fail closed

Missing tax rate pack halts startup — never silently process as zero-tax.

7. Pillar 6 — Three-way reconciliation & settlement

In plain English

Every day we prove our books match the outside world — three statements must tell the same story.

8. Pillar 7 — Transaction risk, fraud & velocity

  • Velocity — e.g. max 3 transactions / minute → automated 15-minute cool-off.
  • PIN failures — 5 wrong PINs in a row → LOCKED_FAILED_PIN.
  • Mule patterns — detect rapid pass-through (money in and straight out within seconds).

Related KYC journeys: new device J9, monitoring J10, cash channels J19.

9. Pillar 8 — Support, disputes & account holds

  • Judicial / administrative freeze — court or central-bank order → FROZEN_JUDICIAL (blocks outbound transfers).
  • Erroneous transfer reversals — recipient consent required before reversing misdirected transfers, unless proven fraud.

10. Pillar 9 — Card management (CMS & 3DS)

Virtual and physical debit cards (Visa, Mastercard, PayPak, mada):

  1. Instant virtual card
  2. Physical personalization
  3. Courier delivery
  4. In-app activation

11. Pillar 10 — Regulatory reporting (STR / CTR)

  • STR — Suspicious Transaction Reports to FIUs (e.g. FMU in Pakistan, SAFIU in Saudi) when structuring / laundering patterns appear.
  • CTR — Currency Transaction Reports for large amounts (example threshold: > 2,000,000 PKR).
  • 10-year retention — keep compliance logs and verification cases immutably for ~10 years.

Deep dive for Pakistan auditors: SBP audit readiness.

  • Position accounts — FX position accounts in the ledger keep multi-currency double-entry balanced.

Section 1 — What is an EMI?

An Electronic Money Institution (EMI) is a non-bank financial entity regulated by the central bank (in Pakistan: State Bank of Pakistan under EMI Regulations). It issues electronic money, stores customer funds in digital wallets, processes payments, and may issue branded debit cards (Visa / Mastercard / PayPak).

1.1 Key operational constraints

Stored value — no interest

EMI wallets are non-interest-bearing stored-value accounts. Funds are safeguarded — not bank deposits earning return.

No lending from deposits

EMIs cannot issue credit cards, overdrafts, or loans funded from customer e-money balances.

Onboarding screens & backend APIs

Step-by-step UI sequence and the APIs that run behind each screen.

In plain English

The customer walks through phone → OTP → PIN → CNIC camera → silent government/telco checks → liveness selfie → profile form → approved or “under review.” Each screen has one clear backend job. 

flowchart TD
    A[1.1 Mobile number] --> B[1.2 OTP verify]
    B --> C[2.1 Set MPIN]
    C --> D[3.1 CNIC front/back OCR]
    D --> E[4.1 Silent NADRA + PMD]
    E --> F[5.1 Liveness + face match]
    F --> G[6.1 Profile + AML screen]
    G --> H{Clean?}
    H -->|Yes| I[6.2 Approved · wallet]
    H -->|Flagged| J[Under review · EDD/PEP]

Phase 1 — Authentication & ownership

Screen 1.1 — Mobile number input

  • UI: Phone field with country code (+92).
  • Backend: Create cryptographically secure 6-digit OTP; send via licensed SMS gateway (Infobip / Twilio / local aggregator).
  • API: POST /api/v1/auth/send-otp

Screen 1.2 — OTP verification

  • UI: 6-digit OTP, auto-focus, 60-second resend timer.
  • Backend: Validate OTP against stored hash; issue temporary JWT registration_token; register device fingerprint.
  • API: POST /api/v1/auth/verify-otp
  • Rule: Wrong OTP never advances state (see J1 / fail-closed habits).

Phase 2 — Credentials & PIN

Screen 2.1 — MPIN creation

  • UI: 4- or 6-digit PIN + confirm.
  • Backend: Salted hash with Argon2id or bcrypt (work factor ≥ 12). Plain-text PIN never stored or logged.
  • API: POST /api/v1/user/set-pin

Phase 3 — Identity document capture (OCR)

Screen 3.1 — CNIC front & back

  • UI: Camera frame with glare/edge feedback and auto-capture.
  • Backend: Encrypt upload to object storage (e.g. S3); OCR parses CNIC number, name, DOB, issue & expiry dates.
  • API: POST /api/v1/kyc/cnic-ocr

Phase 4 — Government & telco verification (silent)

Screen 4.1 — Background processing / read-only review

  • UI: Progress (“Verifying with official databases…”). Optionally show non-editable OCR fields for sanity check before paid lookups.
  • Backend:
    1. NADRA Verisys — authenticity, demographics / official photo.
    2. PMD telco match — mobile number owned by that CNIC.
  • API: POST /api/v1/kyc/verify-identity

Phase 5 — Liveness & facial biometrics

Screen 5.1 — Active liveness & selfie

  • UI: Oval guide; blink / turn head / keep steady.
  • Backend:
    1. Liveness engine — block masks, screen replays, paper photos.
    2. Face match — CNN embeddings vs NADRA/CNIC face; example threshold ≥ 85%.
  • API: POST /api/v1/kyc/liveness-facematch
  • Product tiering (SRS): Completing this may upgrade Tier 0 → Tier 1. Skipping may leave Tier 0 — confirm against CCOF live-photo requirements for digital EMI.
  • Important: Face match / liveness is not NADRA fingerprint BV. Fingerprint BV is the biometric ladder for higher EMI bands (ladder).

Phase 6 — Compliance screening & provisioning

Screen 6.1 — Additional profile details

  • UI: Address, occupation / source of income, email, mother’s maiden name (and other Table-A / EMI fields as required).
  • Backend: AML + sanctions (UNSC, OFAC, local proscribed) + PEP screen; on clean status provision Stored Value Account (SVA) in core.
  • API: POST /api/v1/kyc/submit-profile

Screen 6.2 — Final status

  • UI: Success for instant approval, or “Under review” for flagged accounts.
  • API: GET /api/v1/kyc/status
  • Cooling-off: Pakistan digital banking controls still expect the 2-hour wait before full operation — see activation.

Drop-off handling & incomplete KYC

How the app remembers where the customer stopped — and how we re-check security before continuing.

In plain English

If the app crashes or the customer leaves, we save a draft under the same phone / application. We do not dump half-finished CNIC photos into the final customer table forever. After long gaps we force OTP / liveness again so a stranger cannot finish someone else’s pack. 

flowchart TD
    A[User re-opens app] --> B[GET /api/v1/onboarding/state]
    B --> C{Status}
    C -->|APPROVED| D[Wallet dashboard]
    C -->|PENDING| E[Under review screen]
    C -->|DRAFT| F[Resume at current_step]

3.1 Database strategy

Keep incomplete data in draft storage (PostgreSQL JSONB and/or Redis) so unverified profiles do not pollute production customer tables.

3.2 Drop-off security rules

  1. TTL cleanup — S3 lifecycle deletes unverified ID images after ~30 days.
  2. Re-verification window — if idle > 24 hours, re-trigger sensitive steps (OTP and/or fresh liveness) before showing draft.

Edge cases

Device change mid-onboarding

New device fingerprint → invalidate old session → OTP ownership proof → then load draft on Phone B.

OCR / liveness retry exhaustion

After repeated failures, send tips / support chat (“glare-free photo”) instead of a generic nudge only.

Privacy reason

Do not keep unverified identity documents forever without active consent — purge drafts on the 30-day TTL.

PEP verification & EDD mechanics

Politically Exposed Persons can be onboarded — but never with a silent auto-approve. Journey mapping: J6.

Who is a PEP?

Someone with a prominent public role (e.g. MNA/MPA, senior judge, military general, SOE executive), plus close family and associates. They need Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) and senior sign-off. 

flowchart TD
    A[User submits KYC profile] --> B[Screening · fuzzy PEP match]
    B -->|No match| C[Standard path]
    B -->|Match| D[Status = PENDING_EDD]
    D --> E[App: under compliance review]
    E --> F[EDD form · SoW / office details]
    F --> G[Compliance officer review]
    G --> H[MLRO sign-off]
    H --> I[Activate as High Risk · enhanced monitoring]

4.1 Workflow rules

  • Fuzzy match against PEP database — do not require exact spelling only.
  • Match → PENDING_EDD; app shows under-review copy (no tip-off of list names).
  • Push dynamic EDD form (source of wealth, public office details).
  • Webhook / case to Compliance Admin dashboard.
  • Officer rules out false positives (father name / CNIC / DOB).
  • MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer) approves; account marked high-risk for ongoing monitoring (J10).

4.2 Fuzzy matching algorithms

AlgorithmBest for
Jaro-WinklerShort human names; rewards shared prefixes
LevenshteinEdit distance (insert/delete/substitute)
Soundex / MetaphonePhonetic matches (how the name sounds)

Typical similarity threshold in this SRS: about 75–80% to catch transliterations (Muhammad vs Mohammad).

4.3 Audit trail

  • Log every MLRO action, notes, and SoW attachments.
  • Immutable audit table; retain ~10 years for regulatory exams.
Never auto-approve a PEP match

False-positive clearing still needs a human. True PEPs need EDD + MLRO — see also SBP audit readiness.

Compliance → Cases

  • Workspace for PEP / sanctions-flagged files.
  • Request/review Source of Wealth; internal notes; route to MLRO digital sign-off.

Compliance → Alerts

  • Duplicate CNIC / SIM flags.
  • Multiple failed biometric / liveness attempts.
